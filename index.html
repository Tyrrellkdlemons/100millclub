How to use the terminal

1 · Pick a market mode, then pick something to trade

The tabs at the top — All markets, Futures, Stocks, Crypto, Forex, Indices — steer the whole terminal: watchlist, chart, ticker tape, screener, heatmap and the signals desk all follow the mode you pick. Then click any row in the Watchlist to load it.

The search box (or /) opens the big search: fuzzy matching over your whole board, class tabs, favourites and recents — plus a live hunt across thousands more stocks, coins, pairs, futures and indices. Pick anything from the long tail and it joins your universe permanently: it charts, gets real prices where obtainable, demo-trades and takes alerts like a built-in. Type ? in the search for its syntax.

2 · Live chart or simulated chart

The Live toggle shows the real TradingView chart with genuine market data and its own drawing and indicator toolbars. Simulated switches to the built-in engine, which is what powers this dashboard's own indicators, drawings and backtests — and it keeps working with no internet connection.

3 · Read the chart

Green bars closed up, red bars closed down. The timeframe buttons (1m → 1w) change how much time each bar covers. Indicators adds average lines and a momentum panel. Drawing tools lets you mark a trend line or a price level. Fullscreen makes the chart fill the screen.

4 · Place a practice order

On the right, choose BUY or SELL, set how many units, then optionally add a stop loss (your safety exit) and a take profit (your target exit). Place the order and it appears under Open positions with live profit and loss. Stops and targets fire automatically as prices move.

5 · Test a strategy before risking anything

Switch to Test a strategy, pick a rule set and a date range, then Run backtest. You get total return, win rate, number of trades, Sharpe ratio, worst drawdown, the final balance, an equity curve against buy & hold, and a full trade log.

6 · The bottom section

Vlogs holds video drops — each card has share buttons for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and X. Technicals, News, Screener, Heatmap and Calendar are live TradingView panels with real market data. Collapse the whole section with the caret, or hide it with the Vlogs button in the top bar.

6½ · The signals desk

The Signals tab in the bottom section runs four desks over real price history — TrendCatcher (EMA posture + ADX), Momentum (RSI + MACD), Mean reversion (Bollinger position) and Volume (OBV) — and shows their combined lean with a confidence bar. One tap opens the full reasoning, plus ATR-derived stop and target levels sized the way professionals size them. Cards are ranked by what you actually watch. Where no live feed is reachable a card says Simulated in plain sight. An optional AI read narrates the indicators — via the site's AI if configured, or your own OpenRouter key from the Coach's AI desk. All of it teaches method; none of it is financial advice.

Under the cards sits the pro shelf — the calendars, screeners, sentiment dials and liquidation maps real desks read, curated per market mode, opening in new tabs. And the Bot lab card jumps to the built-in backtester: replay any rule set on history before you trust it with anything.

6¾ · My Desk and the commodities board

My Desk (bottom section, or press D) is your own room: equity and day P/L up top, then the numbers real journals grade by — win rate, profit factor, expectancy in R (profit measured in units of what the stop risked), max drawdown and streaks — the equity curve full-width, the open book and working orders as proper tables, a journal with a notes column that syncs with your cloud account, per-market results, a browser-local position calculator, and the risk guard: a daily loss limit run the way funded desks run one. Hit it and new entries are refused until tomorrow — closing what is open always stays allowed.

In Futures and All-markets modes, the Signals tab also carries the commodities intelligence board: CFTC Commitments of Traders positioning (who is long, who is short, how crowded), the futures curve per commodity (contango or backwardation, and what rolling costs per year), the macro drivers from FRED (rates, dollar, inflation expectations), and 7-day ag-belt weather. Every card names its primary source and its freshness, and a Plain English panel decodes the jargon. Signals themselves got stricter too: only confirmed closes vote, stale data and hard desk disagreement veto a signal outright — and the veto log shows what got refused and why.

7 · Using your own TradingView account

The charts and market panels built into this page are TradingView's public embeds. They always load anonymously — they run in a sandboxed frame that cannot read your tradingview.com login, so they cannot show your subscription's real-time feeds no matter who is signed in. That is a TradingView platform rule, not a setting here.

To see something on your own plan, use the blue TradingView button in the chart toolbar (or in the panel header). It opens the exact symbol and timeframe you are looking at on tradingview.com. If you are already signed in there, it loads with everything you pay for — real-time entitled data, your saved layouts, your indicators and your alerts. If you are not signed in, you simply get the normal free view.

This is also why the index charts here use TradingView's own index feeds rather than the exchange tickers: the exchange versions need a paid data entitlement and refuse to load in an embed. The TradingView button still sends you to the entitled ticker, so subscribers get the real feed.
